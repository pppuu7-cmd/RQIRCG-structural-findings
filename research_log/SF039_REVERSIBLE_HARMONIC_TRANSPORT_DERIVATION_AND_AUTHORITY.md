## 1. Exact phase-degree theorem

For one body with

`H(t)=p^2/(2m)+(m omega^2/2)[x-Q(t)]^2`,

and frozen center program

`Q(t)=q0+d f(t)`,

the forced-harmonic propagator is an ordinary oscillator evolution followed by a phase-space displacement and a c-number phase whose dependence on the prescribed forcing trajectory is at most quadratic.

For a fixed common `f(t)` and branch-independent `omega`, the branch-dependent c-number phase may therefore be written generically as

`phi_I(d_I)=const_I + alpha_I d_I + beta_I d_I^2`,

where `alpha_I,beta_I` may depend on the pulse shape, duration, frequency and reference center but not on the Boolean branch labels except through `d_I`.

Preparation plus inverse/reversed recombination has the same degree bound: composition changes the coefficients and displacement closure conditions, not the polynomial degree in each prescribed branch displacement.

## 2. Frozen COM-closed drives

`d_A=lambda a ell`,

`d_B=2 b ell`,

`d_C=3 c ell`,

`d_D=-(lambda a+2b+3c)ell/5`.

The D body is the nontrivial shared-control case.

Using Boolean identities `a^2=a`, `b^2=b`, `c^2=c`,

`(lambda a+2b+3c)^2`

reduces exactly to

`lambda^2 a +4b+9c+4lambda ab+6lambda ac+12bc`.

There is no `abc` term.

Therefore the full control phase

`Phi_ctrl=sum_I [alpha_I d_I+beta_I d_I^2]`

is a Boolean polynomial containing only constant, one-body and pairwise monomials.

The exact connected Boolean difference is consequently

`Delta3 Phi_ctrl = 0`.

This includes all cross terms generated by the common recoil body.

## 3. Exact symbolic control

An independent symbolic expansion evaluated the generic coefficients `alpha_I,beta_I` without choosing them and returned

`Delta3_generic_quadratic = 0`.

Under a common multiplicative amplitude error

`lambda -> (1+g)lambda`,

the same calculation gives

`Delta3_gain_error = 0`.

Adding an explicit cubic counterterm

`chi a b c`

gives

`Delta3 = chi`,

as required for the negative control.

No coefficient was fitted.

## 4. Body-local offsets

Let a body-local center program contain an additional branch-independent offset `u_I(t)`.

The quadratic phase functional generates terms of the form

`u_I^2`, `u_I d_I`, and `d_I^2`.

Because `u_I` carries no branch label and every `d_I` is linear in `(a,b,c)`, this adds only degree 0,1,2 Boolean terms.

Hence the connected phase remains zero.

## 5. Signed reversal

`lambda -> -lambda` flips the sign of all terms containing one power of the A drive and leaves even powers unchanged.

The Boolean degree never exceeds two. Therefore the ideal reversible transport remains connected-null under either sign.

This provides a physical implementation class for the SF038 signed-amplitude operation at the ideal quadratic-control level.

## 6. Minimum evasion content

The theorem can be evaded only by adding new control content outside the frozen quadratic/linear-drive class, for example:

- a center/force term proportional to `abc`;
- a genuine three-register interaction `hbar chi n_A n_B n_C`;
- branch-dependent trap parameters whose dependence contains a degree-three Boolean component;
- nonlinear cross-body actuator response that produces a degree-three branch polynomial.

A generic anharmonic potential alone does not automatically imply a connected phase; the relevant question is whether its branch/control dependence produces degree-three content after the complete preparation/readout sequence.

Thus retain:

`NONQUADRATIC != AUTOMATIC_CONNECTED_CONTAMINATION`.

## 7. External physical-realization authority

The implementation class is physically non-vacuous.

E. Torrontegui et al., “Fast atomic transport without vibrational heating,” arXiv:1010.3271, uses dynamical invariants for transport in a one-dimensional moving harmonic trap and shows that appropriate trap trajectories can perform fast transport without final vibrational heating.

D. Guery-Odelin and J. G. Muga, “Transport in a harmonic trap: shortcuts to adiabaticity and robust protocols,” arXiv:1410.4957, derives exact final excitation-energy expressions and robust transport protocols for moving harmonic traps.

P. C. Haljan et al., “Spin-dependent forces on trapped ions for phase-stable quantum gates and motional Schrodinger-cat states,” arXiv:quant-ph/0411068, experimentally demonstrates state-dependent forces coupling qubit/internal states to motion.

These sources motivate the transport class only. They do not validate a specific RQIRCGSF device, mass scale, force technology or error budget.
