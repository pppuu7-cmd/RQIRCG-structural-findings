## A — gauge-algebra closure

The inherited massless spin-2 gauge structure strongly constrains any nonlinear continuation. A self-consistent interaction cannot arbitrarily violate the source identity/constraint structure that made the linear carrier gauge-redundant and compatible with conserved sources.

However uniqueness of the *deformed gauge algebra* is not equivalent to uniqueness of the *full invariant action/generator*. Once a nonlinear gauge symmetry exists, one may ask whether additional functionals are separately invariant under that same symmetry.

Therefore the decisive SF003 test is lane B.

## B — invariant-vertex escape: explicit structural counterexample

Consider a metric-like nonlinear carrier `g = eta + kappa h` after a candidate gauge closure has promoted the linear redundancy to a diffeomorphism-like nonlinear redundancy. Any local scalar density built covariantly from the resulting curvature is invariant under that redundancy when integrated with the invariant measure, modulo boundary terms.

A schematic cubic-curvature interaction

`I_3 = integral d^4x sqrt(-g) [Riemann x Riemann x Riemann]`

provides the relevant counterexample class.

Around a flat background, curvature begins at first order in `h`. Therefore a cubic-curvature invariant begins at order `h^3` (or higher after expansion of the measure) and does not alter the frozen quadratic kinetic operator at leading flat-background order. Yet it supplies new nonlinear vertices. With an independent coefficient `alpha`,

`S_alpha = S_base + alpha I_3`,

all members share the same leading quadratic massless spin-2 carrier while differing in nonlinear interaction structure.

This is exactly the structure SF003 preregistered as a falsifier:

1. begins above quadratic order — YES;
2. invariant under the nonlinear gauge redundancy — YES at the covariant functional level;
3. preserves the leading quadratic carrier — YES for a curvature-cubic term about flat space;
4. modifies nonlinear response — YES generically;
5. carries an independent coefficient not fixed by the lower-order quadratic calibration — YES absent an additional principle.

No value of `alpha` is selected here and no claim is made that arbitrary cubic-curvature theories satisfy every health condition. Their existence as an invariant interaction direction is sufficient to defeat gauge closure as a complete selector.

## C — physical-equivalence quotient

The counterexample is not the 4D Gauss-Bonnet quadratic boundary combination already identified in G60. Generic cubic curvature invariants are not all removable by a boundary term or a simple invertible local field redefinition. The exact independent basis is not needed for the present decision: SF003 requires only one genuinely inequivalent invariant nonlinear direction to survive the quotient.

External literature confirms that cubic-curvature gravities and EFT curvature-cubic operators can leave linearized flat-background behavior unchanged while appearing first in nonlinear observables. This confirmation was not used to formulate the preregistered falsification test.

## D — causal/constraint health does not rescue selector power by itself

Specific higher-curvature directions may fail stability, unitarity, hyperbolicity, or positive-operational requirements. But NGC would become a selector only if the already-frozen principles excluded *all* inequivalent invariant directions. No such theorem follows from gauge closure alone.

Indeed, there exist specially constructed higher-curvature theories whose linearized spectrum around selected backgrounds contains only the usual graviton, demonstrating that even a simple “extra term implies extra linear mode” rejection rule is not universally valid. Health conditions must therefore be audited rather than assumed to collapse the space uniquely.
