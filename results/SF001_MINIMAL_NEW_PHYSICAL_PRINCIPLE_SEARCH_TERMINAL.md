## Adversarial nonuniqueness argument against P-A

Let a closed generator `S_0` reproduce the validated quadratic massless spin-2 weak-field sector and the lower-order source coupling. If the principle only requires a single local closed generator, then adding an independently admissible local nonlinear invariant `I[g,psi,...]` with coefficient `alpha`, chosen so that it begins beyond the frozen quadratic/leading weak-field order,

`S_alpha = S_0 + alpha I`,

can preserve the same inherited lower-order structure while changing nonlinear response. The exact available family depends on the admissible DOF, derivative order, symmetries, and state-space assumptions; the existence of this structural escape is enough to show that “one generator” alone is not a selector.

This is the generator-level analogue of NP1: the location of underdetermination has moved, but it has not disappeared.

No claim is made here that every `I` is healthy, causal, or physically allowed. The point is precisely that additional admissibility information is needed to decide which are allowed.

## External consistency cross-check — not a selection input

After the internal audit was frozen conceptually, standard massless spin-2 consistency results were used only as a sanity check. Deser-style self-coupling arguments show that locality and consistency can recursively generate Einstein-type nonlinearities from the conserved current of the linear gauge system. BRST deformation results likewise show very strong rigidity under assumptions such as locality and at most two derivatives; for collections of massless spin-2 fields the admissible deformations reduce to separate Einstein-Hilbert sectors with cosmological terms rather than arbitrary cross-couplings.

This external convergence is informative but cannot be imported as the principle. It instead identifies exactly which extra assumptions have selection power: derivative order, propagating content, locality/gauge-deformation class, and absence/presence of new scales or fields.
