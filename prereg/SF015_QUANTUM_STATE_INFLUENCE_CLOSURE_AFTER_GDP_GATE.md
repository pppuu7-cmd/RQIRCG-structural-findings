## Context

## Scientific question

Conditional on a fixed Einstein-Hilbert-like local classical generator, do the inherited relational/closed/GDP principles uniquely determine the quantum gravitational state and influence functional needed for operational predictions?

## Frozen distinction

Separate three objects:

1. **local classical generator** `Gamma_cl`;
2. **quantization/measure/state datum** `Q = {measure, contour, state rho_g, boundary prescription, renormalization data}`;
3. **operational influence object** obtained after integrating/conditioning the gravitational sector with `Q`.

SF015 asks whether fixing object 1 by GDP also fixes object 2 and hence object 3.

## Positive criterion

Return

`GDP_PLUS_INHERITED_STRUCTURE_FIXES_QUANTUM_INFLUENCE_SCOPED`

only if the existing principles force a unique physically relevant `Q` (up to equivalence) in the target weak-field closed-source domain.

## Counterexample-first failure criterion

Return

`CLASSICAL_GENERATOR_SELECTION_DOES_NOT_FIX_QUANTUM_STATE_INFLUENCE_DATA`

if two inequivalent choices of admissible `Q` can share the same local classical generator and inherited weak-field retarded response while producing different higher correlators/noise/influence data.

A decisive witness need not compute `chi_ABC`; it is enough to show nonuniqueness of the parent operational object before that projection.

## Frozen minimal control

For a fixed quadratic bosonic field generator, vacuum, thermal, squeezed, coherent, and other admissible Gaussian states share the same canonical commutator/retarded Green function structure while their symmetrized/Hadamard covariance generally differs.

SF015 must determine whether the closed relational gravitational architecture eliminates that state freedom or merely constrains it.

## Anti-shortcut rule

Choosing “the vacuum” is not a derived selector unless the relevant vacuum and boundary prescription are independently fixed by the physical architecture. Likewise “no incoming radiation” is a boundary/state condition, not a consequence of local equations alone.
